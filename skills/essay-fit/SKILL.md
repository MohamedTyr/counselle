# Essay Fit

Source: `plans/research-counselor-judgment.md` (fit/positioning; named-entity discipline).

## The hidden decision

"Help me with Northwestern's community essay" is not a request for prose. It is
"which real, specific things at *this* school let me show I belong and will
contribute, given what I've actually done?" A generic "find a community you're
passionate about" answer fails. The value is in naming the actual organizations,
programs, traditions, and courses at this school that a reader will recognize and
that genuinely connect to the student's activity — then framing the contribution.

## Evidence plan

Use `counselor-research` for source routing. In one round after `resolve_school`:

- `.edu` for real, current student organizations, programs, traditions, living-learning
  communities, and courses that map to the student's activity.
- Broad web for the school's own descriptions of its culture and distinctive programs.
- Reddit (`{school}` sub + ApplyingToCollege) for what students actually say those
  communities are like, and which ones are real and active versus dormant.

## Fingerprint this school first (unknown until searched)

The named specifics are the entire deliverable, and they are **unknown until you
search this school** — never generate them from memory:

- **Real, currently-existing named orgs that match the student's activity.** Take the
  activity (e.g. decorating the school) and search for this school's actual matching
  communities — spirit/traditions organizations, event and homecoming committees,
  arts and design groups, service orgs — by name: `"[school] student organizations
  [theme]"`, `"[school] traditions clubs"`, `{school}` subreddit. Recommend only
  groups a search actually surfaced.
- **The real name, exactly.** A group's real branding often differs from the obvious
  guess — verify what it actually calls itself and quote it as returned; if no real
  match surfaces, describe the *category* honestly rather than inventing a plausible
  club name.
- **Distinctive school-specific programs and language** the essay can hook into
  (named traditions, a signature program, a course), so the essay reads as *this*
  school and not a find-and-replace of another.

## Deductions and traps

- **Never invent a club, program, tradition, or community.** A hallucinated name is a
  factual lie and a credibility risk if a reader knows the school — this is the
  named-entity discipline, non-negotiable.
- **Contribution, not just membership.** The essay must show what the student *adds*,
  tied to the activity they actually did — match the real org to real, demonstrated
  experience, never manufactured passion.
- **Do not fabricate the student's own facts.** If you lack the real detail that makes
  the connection specific, interview for it before drafting.
- **Specific beats prestigious.** A small, real, well-matched community the student
  can speak to credibly beats naming the school's most famous institute they have no
  connection to.

## Final answer shape

1. Name the 2–4 real, verified communities/programs that best fit the student's
   activity, each with the concrete tie to what they've done.
2. Frame the contribution: what the student brings, in their own real experience.
3. Separate confirmed campus facts (cited) from any uncertainty about a group's
   current activity.
4. End with the next move (which to feature, what real detail to add, or what to
   ask the student for).

If a search does not surface a real named match, say so and give the honest
category-level direction instead of a fabricated name.

## Exemplar shape

A student writing Northwestern's community-and-contribution essay whose activity is
decorating their school. The veteran move is not "write about loving community" — it
is to search Northwestern's actual spirit, traditions, and event organizations, name
the real ones that fit school-decorating and event design, quote them correctly, and
show how that specific student would contribute to those specific groups.
