# Majors and Fit

Source: `plans/research-counselor-judgment.md` (major/career-driven playbook; comparison principles).

## The hidden decision

"Which school is better for CS?" and "Should I apply as anthropology instead of
business?" are not the same problem. First identify which decision the student is
making: admission strategy; curriculum and department access; career or grad-school
prep; freedom to explore or switch; identity and narrative coherence; or best school
after cost and environment. Do not answer one axis as though it resolves the others —
a department can be excellent but inaccessible, and a strategic major can nudge review
while making the whole file less credible.

## Read the student before the program

Establish: strongest/most advanced coursework (and what the school offered); sustained
activities, work, research, or family responsibilities; intended career only to the
degree they truly know it; theory vs applied and large vs small preferences; likely
need to switch or combine fields; and cost constraints, especially for paths needing
grad school. The goal is an honest academic home that doesn't block plausible next
steps — not a permanent identity forced on a seventeen-year-old.

## Evidence plan

Use `counselor-research` for routing. In the same evidence round: DB (`get_domain`,
`get_school_profile`) for structure, rates, outcomes; official sources for admission
channel, direct-entry, transfer, prerequisites, declaration timing; broad web for
current expert context; Reddit for applicant-reported major access and rigor mismatch.

## Fingerprint this school first (unknown until searched)

Which schools are strong for a field, and what a given school actually rewards, are
**not knowable from prestige or from memory** — discover them for the specific field
and schools asked. Two modes:

**Field-strength mode** ("where should someone study [field]?", niche or
pre-professional fields). Top programs are unknown until searched, and **prestige is
not strength**: do not default to the famous general-purpose names (Harvard, MIT,
Stanford, Cornell) for a specialized field. Search `"best undergraduate programs for
[field]"`, specialist/departmental rankings, land-grant / conservatory / dedicated
schools, and the relevant subreddit (`CollegeMajors`, a field sub, `{school}` subs).
Recommend the schools the *field* rates, with the real reason each is strong.

**Archetype mode** ("what does school X look for?", profile-building, "MIT vs Harvard
vs …"). Each selective school builds toward a **distinct** applicant archetype — verify
it per school and keep them distinct; never blur several into one generic "be excellent
and well-rounded". Triangulate from: the school's `important factors` in CDS if
available, its admissions/mission language (`.edu`, `"what [school] values in
applicants"`), AO interviews and reputable analysis (broad web), and a Reddit **sweep
of accepted-profile threads** (`{school}` + ApplyingToCollege + chanceme): `"accepted
[school] profile"`, `"[school] what got me in"`. Produce a separate, concrete
fingerprint per school named — the differences between peers are the whole answer.

**Access, both modes** (`.edu`): direct-entry vs internal-transfer rules,
prerequisites, declaration timing — the structure that can block the plan.

## Deductions

- **Intended major matters without formal admit-by-major.** Colleges still build
  classes and read academic claims for credibility; CS, engineering, business, nursing,
  architecture face more pressure than the institution-wide rate suggests.
- **Direct admission changes the risk.** Getting into the university may not grant
  access to the desired school or major; a moderate overall rate can be a reach for a
  capped program. Verify the channel.
- **Switching in later may be the hardest route.** Never recommend an easier college or
  major assuming transfer is routine — verify prerequisites, GPA gates, seat limits, and
  whether internal transfer is even permitted.
- **Strategic-major mismatch is a credibility problem.** A file that screams CS/business
  while naming an unrelated field to dodge competition reads as calculation. A genuine
  pivot needs evidence: coursework, projects, reading, experiences.
- **Pre-med is not a major.** The real comparison is prerequisite access, advising,
  research/clinical opportunity, grading environment, and cost — a lower-cost school
  where the student thrives can beat a prestigious one that brings debt or weak grades.
- **Major name is not curriculum, and career fit includes fallbacks.** Same-labelled
  programs differ in theory, computation, labs, and flexibility; for licensure-heavy or
  volatile fields, prefer programs with multiple viable exits. Compare access, not
  branding.

## Traps

- "Best for X" from prestige/rankings without defining the student's tradeoff.
- Assuming a famous department means undergraduates get its courses, faculty, or
  recruiting.
- Treating declared-major policy as stable across cycles or colleges within a university.
- Optimizing for first-job salary while ignoring cost, completion risk, grad-school
  plans, and learning environment — or forcing certainty when exploration is a real fit
  requirement.

## Final answer shape

Begin by naming the axis (admission risk, curriculum, access, outcomes, flexibility, or
identity), give the student-specific judgment, verify the structure that could block the
plan, and end with a move.

1. State the recommendation on the major-school fit question first.
2. Give the 2–4 reasons that determine it.
3. Separate official requirements from applicant-reported patterns.
4. Name one decisive caveat only if it changes action.
5. End with the immediate next step.

For comparisons, end conditionally: "Choose A if direct access to X and a structured
path matter most; choose B if cost, exploration, and combining Y with Z matter more." A
mediocre answer lists majors, repeats rankings, or says "follow your passion".

## Exemplar shapes

- **Arts-heavy activities with pre-med plans:** the student isn't applying "to pre-med."
  Apply with the honest profile, choose a home fitting demonstrated strengths, preserve
  prerequisite and advising access.
- **Different major to dodge CS/business competition:** don't manufacture a pivot; if the
  file screams one field and names another, the mismatch can hurt.
- **Does intended major affect chances?** Often yes in crowded fields — classify the
  program-level risk and verify switch-in realism before recommending the school.
